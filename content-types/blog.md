# Blog posts and community stories

| i18n type id | English, in `exercism/blog` |
|---|---|
| `blog-post` | `posts/<slug>.md` |
| `community-story` | `stories/<slug>.md` |

A post's title, description and marketing copy, and a story's title and blurb, live in the
repo's `config.json` and are not part of this type (fragment text, see `START-HERE.md`).

## What these files look like

- Plain Markdown with **no frontmatter**, and usually no `#` title either: the file starts
  with the first paragraph ("Hi everyone,"). Do not add a title.
- Written in the first person by a named person, usually a founder or staff member, to the
  community. **This is the one content type where the author's personal voice is the point.**
  Keep it personal, direct and warm; keep "I" as "I" and "we" as "we". Do not flatten it into
  corporate announcement prose, and do not make it more excitable than it is.
- Posts are dated and stay as they were written. A post about a 2023 challenge is translated
  as a post about a 2023 challenge; nothing is updated, corrected or modernised.
- Community stories are transcripts or write-ups of interviews. A person's quoted words are
  translated as speech, in the register that person would plausibly use.

## Translate

- Everything that is prose, including image alt text and link text.
- Idiom by meaning. English blog prose is full of it ("blown away", "reach out", "a big
  deal"); a literal rendering is the most common way these posts go wrong.

## Leave alone

- Hashtags and campaign names exactly as written: `#12in23`, `#48in24`. They are what people
  search for and post with.
- People's names, handles, company and product names.
- Embedded HTML, video embeds and their attributes; only an `alt` or visible text inside is
  translated.
- URLs, including links to English-only pages and videos. Do not add "(in English)".
